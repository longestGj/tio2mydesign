## 2. Keyword and intent evidence

The natural seed is `titanium dioxide for powder coatings`, with useful research variations around TiO2 selection, extrusion, cure, opacity, gloss, flow, film thickness and overbake colour shift. The original-query sample contains multiple dedicated application pages plus manufacturer portfolio and technical-guide results. It supports a real application-specific content task, although general-coatings results and unrelated meanings of `powder` remain present.

The commercial expansion is too polluted to justify a supplier-led article. The article should therefore answer a technical comparison question first and lead to documents and a controlled trial second.

## 3. Difference from the approved Coatings parent

The approved APP-COAT article already states that liquid evidence cannot be transferred to powder coatings and briefly cites ASTM D3451. It does not explain the complete powder-specific chain:

`premix → melt extrusion and pigment distribution → cooling/grinding/classification → powder application → film build → cure → finished-film endpoints`

R03 can add genuine depth by showing why a candidate cannot be judged from dry-pigment data or one favourable opacity/gloss number. It can distinguish hiding at controlled film thickness, colour change through the declared cure, gloss/flow/leveling/surface texture, powder/application behaviour, and mechanical/durability endpoints.

## 4. Technical evidence feasibility

- ASTM D3451-24 supplies the governing caution: method choice and interpretation depend on the application, purchaser-seller agreement and often powder chemistry; some tests are relative comparisons.
- ASTM D6441-05(2024) makes cured film thickness part of powder-coating hiding-power comparison. Its public scope is method context, not a universal specification.
- ASTM D5382-02(2025), D7378-16(2024), D5861-07(2025), D4242-25, D4217-25 and D2967-24 provide leads for optical properties, applied-powder thickness, particle size, inclined flow, gel time and corner coverage. D22 must verify current public scope before relying on any one method.
- The 2011 pigment-loading study supports the bounded interaction among TiO2 loading, powder particle size, film thickness and finished appearance.
- The 1999 dispersion study supports extrusion-stage pigment-distribution complexity and warns against treating indirect gloss or opacity as a standalone dispersion measure.

Together, these sources can sustain a substantive explanation without a recipe. Study conditions and manufacturer claims cannot become universal targets or TiO2 Malaysia product claims.

## 5. Evidence limitations

- Search metrics and a controlled local Google SERP are unavailable.
- Full ASTM procedures were not accessed.
- Publisher full-text access is incomplete; paper details must be checked during D22 research before numerical use.
- Powder chemistry, fillers, additives, pigment loading, extrusion conditions, application settings, substrate, cure schedule and film thickness can all affect comparison; no universal control set is proven for every system.
- No current authority establishes named TiO2 Malaysia grade suitability for powder coatings.
- No captured evidence supports universal dosage, fixed processing/cure settings, equivalence, savings, VOC/environmental, regulatory or service-life claims.

## 6. Research conclusion

The evidence is sufficient for one APP-COAT child article centred on fair powder-coating candidate comparison from extrusion through the cured film. It is not sufficient for a formulation guide, a grade-selection catalogue or a separate functional pipe/rebar article.
