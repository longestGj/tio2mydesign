## 2. Working title direction

**Titanium Dioxide for Powder Coatings: How Extrusion, Film Build, Cure, and Finished-Film Endpoints Shape a Fair Comparison**

The exact title may be refined if the reader, owner, decision, explanatory centre and scope remain unchanged.

## 3. Primary reader and decision

Powder-coating formulators, application laboratories, manufacturing and quality teams, and technical procurement teams comparing a pigmentary TiO2 candidate in a declared thermoset powder-coating system.

Help the reader decide which formulation, extrusion, powder-application, film-build, cure and finished-film conditions must be declared and matched before a candidate can be compared fairly with the incumbent.

The intended next action is a bounded request for current documents and matched cured-panel evaluation—not selection of a TiO2 Malaysia Grade from generic marketing language.

## 4. Core question

How should a powder-coating formulator compare TiO2 candidates without treating dry-pigment data or one favourable opacity or gloss value as finished-system proof?

## 5. Required explanatory argument

1. Explain why powder coating is a distinct comparison environment rather than a dry version of liquid coating.
2. Use this explanatory spine: premix → melt extrusion and pigment distribution → cooling, grinding and classification → powder application → controlled film build → cure → separate finished-film endpoints.
3. Explain that extrusion-stage distribution and the full formulation/process history can influence the cured film; do not claim one indirect result proves dispersion quality.
4. Explain why hiding/opacity observations must travel with cured film thickness, substrate/contrast basis, application and measurement conditions.
5. Separate hiding/contrast, initial colour and undertone, colour change through the declared cure or overbake comparison, gloss, flow, leveling, surface texture, powder/application behaviour, and mechanical/durability endpoints.
6. Define a fair matched comparison using the same declared resin/crosslinker chemistry, fillers/additives, pigment loading basis, premix/extrusion history, grind/classification and powder condition, application equipment/settings, substrate, film thickness, cure schedule, conditioning, methods and endpoints.
7. Explain that changing one of those conditions may change the result and prevent a clean pigment-only conclusion.
8. Explain that standards define methods, scope and comparison context; they do not establish TiO2 Grade suitability or a universal acceptance criterion.
9. Treat functional pipe and rebar powder coatings as an explicit non-transfer boundary, not as a second topic or proof branch.
10. End with a concise buyer input checklist for current documents and a matched cured-panel trial.

## 7. Product and claim boundary

Do not name, recommend, rank or imply powder-coating suitability for any TiO2 Malaysia Grade. Current generic APP-COAT relationships do not prove powder-specific Grade fit.

Prohibited:

- universal formulation, TiO2 dosage, resin/crosslinker choice, filler package, extrusion temperature/speed, grind size, spray setting, film-build target, cure schedule or acceptance threshold;
- direct equivalence, drop-in replacement or guaranteed opacity, whiteness, gloss, flow, leveling, colour stability, durability, throughput, transfer efficiency, savings or service life;
- manufacturer or supplier results generalized to all TiO2 pigments or to TiO2 Malaysia;
- study-specific numerical conditions presented as plant targets;
- chloride/sulfate route, rutile/anatase label, surface treatment or particle-size label treated as sufficient finished-film proof;
- environmental, VOC, HSE, regulatory, certification or compliance claims without exact current authority;
- liquid-coating evidence transferred to powder coating, or ordinary decorative powder evidence transferred to functional pipe/rebar systems.
