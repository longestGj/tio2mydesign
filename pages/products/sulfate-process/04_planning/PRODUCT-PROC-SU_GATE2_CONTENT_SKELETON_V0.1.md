### 3.3 What the Sulfate Process Tells You

Purpose: answer the process-definition question in one short block and prevent the category label from being treated as a performance conclusion.

Candidate direction:

> At a general industrial level, the sulfate process treats titanium-bearing feedstock with sulfuric acid, followed by further steps that form and finish the TiO₂ pigment. `Sulfate` and `sulphate` are spelling variants for the same process category. The label identifies a production route; it does not by itself establish the best Grade, application fit, performance, cost, environmental result or equivalence.

The definition receives a precise institutional source note and review date. It remains general process context and does not describe an IKHLAS plant, feedstock or operating configuration.

### 3.4 Explore Sulfate Grades

Purpose: let the buyer reduce the set by application without ranking the five Grades or asking the buyer to review all of them.

Cards remain in the approved neutral order:

1. `M-996` — approved direction: industrial, powder and exterior or interior architectural coatings.
2. `M-2196` — approved direction: solvent-based furniture and industrial paint.
3. `M-108` — approved direction: masterbatch and compounds, polyolefin and PVC film, and plastics requiring high thermal stability.
4. `M-52` — approved direction: printing inks, can coatings and high-gloss interior architectural coatings.
5. `M-2377` — approved direction: coatings, plastics, masterbatch, printing inks and paper.

Each card has one `View [Grade]` link to its exact Grade page. Introductory copy tells the buyer to compare only the candidates relevant to the formulation and process. No M-996/M-2196 comparison, ranking, recommendation, equivalence or replacement claim appears.
