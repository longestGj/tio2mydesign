# R03 Buyer Question, Ownership and Claim Boundary V0.1

## Buyer and decision

Primary reader: powder-coating formulators, application laboratories, manufacturing/quality teams and technical procurement teams comparing a pigmentary TiO2 candidate in a declared thermoset powder-coating system.

Core decision: what formulation, extrusion, application, film-build, cure and endpoint conditions must be declared and matched before a candidate can be compared fairly with the incumbent.

After reading, the buyer should be able to define the comparison system, request current documents and plan matched cured panels. The article must not select a TiO2 Malaysia Grade for the reader.

## Content ownership

- `APP-COAT` owns this application-specific decision.
- The approved Coatings parent remains the broad candidate-comparison page and links down to R03 for powder-specific process-to-film detail.
- Grade pages may provide approved grade facts but do not own the cross-grade powder-evaluation framework.
- Process pages own chloride/sulfate manufacture; R03 must not infer powder-coating performance from production route alone.
- Resources own application-independent explanations of opacity, dispersion, standards and replacement qualification.
- Functional pipe and rebar powder coatings remain an explicit non-transfer boundary, not a new article in R03.

## Required content boundary

The article should explain:

1. why powder coating is a distinct comparison environment;
2. how extrusion and pigment distribution precede powder grinding/application and cure;
3. why cured film thickness must travel with opacity/hiding observations;
4. why gloss, flow, leveling, surface texture and colour shift are separate from hiding;
5. why resin/crosslinker, fillers/additives, pigment loading, extrusion, application, substrate, cure and film build must be declared;
6. why standards define methods and comparison context rather than Grade suitability;
7. what documents and matched panels form a credible next step.

## Prohibited claims

- named TiO2 Malaysia Grade recommendation, ranking or powder-coating suitability;
- universal formulation, TiO2 dosage, resin/crosslinker choice, filler package, extrusion temperature/speed, grind size, spray setting, cure schedule or acceptance threshold;
- direct equivalence, drop-in replacement or guaranteed opacity, whiteness, gloss, flow, leveling, colour stability, durability, throughput, transfer efficiency or savings;
- proprietary supplier results generalized to all TiO2 pigments or to TiO2 Malaysia;
- study-specific conditions presented as plant targets;
- chloride/sulfate route, rutile/anatase label, surface treatment or particle-size label treated as sufficient proof of finished-film performance;
- environmental, VOC, HSE, regulatory, certification or service-life claims without exact current authority;
- liquid-coating evidence transferred to powder, or ordinary decorative powder evidence transferred to functional pipe/rebar systems.

## Reader-quality constraint

The explanatory sequence must dominate. Any comparison checklist is subordinate and short. The article must not read like a qualification SOP, internal governance file, standard-method reproduction, formula sheet or supplier catalogue.
