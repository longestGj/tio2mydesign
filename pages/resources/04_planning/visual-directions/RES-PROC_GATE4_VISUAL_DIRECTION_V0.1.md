## 1. Design Intent

`Process Qualification Ledger` presents chloride-versus-sulfate evaluation as a disciplined movement from route orientation to grade evidence and validation. Its visual hierarchy makes the boundary unmistakable: process labels are useful context, while grade-level conclusions require comparable evidence.

The direction is white-led, sober and information-dense. Navy carries headings and technical authority, Deep Navy contains the direct answer and closing action, and Functional Teal marks navigation, sequence, focus and evidence movement. It deliberately avoids process-score graphics, factory imagery and visual winner cues.

## 4. Component Direction

### 4.1 Hero and Decision Path

- Keep the approved H1/support/action in the 8/4 Desktop split and one-column compact flow.
- Use no image, illustration placeholder or unused media column.
- The four-step ledger is a navigation/decision aid, not new article content or performance evidence.

### 4.2 Direct Answer

- Use a continuous Deep Navy surface and readable white copy.
- Add only a restrained orientation index and Teal rule; do not add a badge or certification metaphor.
- Preserve the normalized statement that route can influence base-pigment formation but the label alone cannot establish grade-level performance.

### 4.3 Route and Label-Boundary Panels

- Chloride and sulfate receive identical width, height behavior, Teal top rule, border and schematic-strip treatment.
- Route arrows are text-level process orientation, not a quantitative diagram.
- The two label-boundary panels may use different role edges, but never red/green color, ticks, crosses, score or preference indicators.

### 4.4 Grade Evidence and Workflow

- The six-row Desktop table is the page's dominant evidence object.
- Compact screens keep each question, evidence and interpretation together as one ledger record.
- Workflow steps use Teal numbered circles; outcomes use Navy top rules so sequence and decision state are visually distinct.

### 4.5 Citations, FAQ and Sources

- Application-overlap examples use citation slips with direct source links and no product imagery.
- FAQ uses native disclosure semantics, thin rules and a visible Functional Teal focus outline.
- Sources use an editorial ledger: authority label, title, claim role, date and descriptive link remain attached.

### 4.6 Global Chrome and Logos

- Consume Global Chrome V0.5 without forking Header, compact Header, Mobile Menu or Footer.
- `Resources` is current on the active navigation surface.
- Use the primary production Logo on every white/light Header or menu position and the reverse monochrome Logo on the Deep Navy Footer.

## 5. Image and Claim Policy

No hero image is required. Do not use stock chemical imagery, factories, production lines, certificates, flags, maps or decorative pigment clouds. Such imagery would imply unsupported manufacturing, capacity, origin or compliance facts and would compete with the evidence-led task.

The design must not add route-wide opacity, undertone, weatherability, environmental, availability, economics or price conclusions. It must not introduce TiO2 Malaysia Grade recommendations, comparison scores, equivalence badges or process superiority cues.

## 6. Responsive Direction

- Desktop keeps the 1240px shell, equal route panels, evidence table and multi-column workflow.
- Tablet keeps compact Global Chrome and converts the wide table into a two-column record grid without horizontal scroll.
- Mobile uses one column, 38px H1, 16px body text, stacked records and full-width actions.
- Mobile Menu open makes background regions inert/hidden, transfers focus to Close and exposes one current `Resources` item.
