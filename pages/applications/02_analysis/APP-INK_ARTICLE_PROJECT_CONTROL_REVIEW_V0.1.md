## 3. Project-control findings

### Article answer and buyer model — PASS

The opening directly answers the selection question: a suitable TiO2 input is the candidate that produces the required white print in the buyer's actual ink chemistry, printing process, substrate, deposited film, drying/cure and end use. The article then consistently applies this sequence:

`Ink chemistry and printing process → Substrate and deposited film → Optical target → Dispersion, rheology and process behavior → Drying or cure and finished print → Controlled incumbent comparison → Cost per accepted printed output`

This is buyer-facing reasoning, not a page or Gate workflow.

### Printing-ink specificity — PASS

The article does not merely reuse Coatings or Plastics prose. It addresses white-ink functions and differentiates flexographic/gravure, screen, paste/offset and tightly constrained inkjet contexts as question sets rather than universal fit claims. It connects TiO2 comparison to:

- deposited-film opacity on the actual substrate;
- vehicle/dispersant-dependent wetting, milling and achieved dispersion;
- fineness-of-grind interpretation;
- method- and shear-history-dependent rheology;
- settling, separation, redispersibility and storage drift;
- metering, transfer, release, lay and print defects;
- drying/cure and end-use-specific finished-print properties;
- controlled laboratory, pilot or production comparison;
- cost at the same accepted printed result.

Potential defects remain multi-cause observations; the article does not automatically assign them to TiO2.

### Buyer Clean and density — PASS

The article contains no version line, internal-link placeholder, section-anchor placeholder, product-routing placeholder, HTML/CMS instruction, module name, route key, internal approval language or D22/D23 governance term. Two compact tables organize printing contexts and TDS interpretation. The four FAQs answer distinct buyer questions without repeating the full article.

### Evidence fit — PASS

The reader-usable source notes appropriately support the technical boundaries:

1. peer-reviewed rutile-TiO2 aggregate-scattering research supports the general aggregation/dependent-scattering explanation and is explicitly bounded as a principle requiring ink-film confirmation;
2. ISO 23498:2022 supports visual-opacity measurement of printed white ink on transparent and opaque substrates;
3. ISO 13655:2017 supports spectral measurement and colorimetric computation across offset, flexography, gravure, screen and digital graphic-arts printing;
4. ISO 1524:2020, confirmed current by ISO in 2026, supports the fineness-of-grind gauge scope without converting it into primary-particle size or storage stability;
5. accountable printing-ink rheology literature supports process-aware non-Newtonian, thixotropic, viscoelastic and tack discussion;
6. ISO 2834-3:2008 supports laboratory preparation of screen-printing test prints for optical and resistance evaluation;
7. ISO 2836:2021 supports defined print-resistance tests and explicitly excludes food-safety and consumer-protection assessment.

No competitor product page or manufacturer portfolio is used as the article's principal technical authority or as evidence of TiO2 Malaysia capability.

### Claim and scope safety — PASS

The article does not assert a named-grade recommendation, universal best grade, equivalence, drop-in replacement, formulation or dosage, loading/viscosity/milling/printing/cure setting, shelf-life value, wear reduction, performance improvement, regulatory approval, saving, ROI, payback or guarantee. Inkjet and wear are discussed only as contexts that require specific evidence and controlled evaluation.

The article owns generic Printing Inks use-case intent without taking the primary role of a grade page, printing-process subpage, diagnostic resource, process page or country-supplier page.
