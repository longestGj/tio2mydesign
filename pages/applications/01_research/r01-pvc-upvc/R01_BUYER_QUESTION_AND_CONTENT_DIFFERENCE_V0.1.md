## Buyer decision

The primary reader is a PVC compounder, rigid-PVC profile or pipe producer, extrusion technical team, application laboratory, quality team or technical buyer deciding whether a pigmentary TiO2 candidate deserves a controlled trial in a defined PVC system.

The decision is not “what does TiO2 do in plastics?” It is:

> Given the PVC type, stabilizer package, colour, finished product, process, exposure and acceptance endpoints, what evidence is needed to decide whether a candidate can enter a matched PVC qualification without assuming that a generic plastics label, a supplier dosage, or a claimed equivalent proves suitability?

## PVC-specific questions that the approved parent does not answer in depth

1. Is the application rigid/unplasticized PVC, flexible/plasticized PVC, a profile, pipe, sheet, siding, conductor or film?
2. Is the finished article white or coloured, interior or exterior, decorative or performance-critical?
3. How should a buyer reason about UV shielding, pigment photoactivity, colour/gloss retention and chalking without collapsing them into one “weather resistance” claim?
4. Why must surface treatment, stabilizer chemistry and finished-product exposure be assessed together?
5. When is controlled chalking versus non-chalking behaviour a question to verify rather than a universal grade label?
6. Which endpoints belong in the trial: initial colour/undertone and opacity, extrusion behaviour, dispersion/surface defects, heat build-up or dimensional response, and weathered colour, gloss and mechanical-property retention?
7. Why are pipe substitution or certification contexts not ordinary one-for-one replacement claims?
8. What information should be sent to a supplier before requesting documents or a sample?

## Difference from the approved APP-PLAS article

The approved parent establishes the general system-first method for plastics: define resin, introduction route, process, specimen, exposure and endpoints; use TDS fields only for shortlisting; compare incumbent and candidate in a matched trial; evaluate cost only at accepted output.

R01 can add a distinct PVC decision layer:

- PVC family split: rigid/uPVC versus flexible/plasticized PVC;
- stabilizer and pigment-surface-treatment interaction as a qualification variable;
- the dual durability problem of shielding the PVC while controlling pigment-driven surface change;
- profile, siding and pipe exposure contexts;
- white versus coloured product and controlled-chalking versus non-chalking questions;
- PVC-specific finished-product endpoints and pipe replacement constraints.

This is substantive enough to avoid a paraphrase of the parent. The article must reuse the parent method briefly and spend most of its space on these PVC-specific decisions.

## Scope shape

- One article should own PVC and uPVC.
- uPVC is treated as the rigid/unplasticized branch within PVC, not as a separate article.
- Outdoor profiles, sheets, siding, pipes and related rigid-PVC products belong as defined contexts inside the same article.
- Flexible PVC belongs as a controlled comparison branch, not as an independent article in R01.
- PVC film and calendered film remain for R02, because film has its own proposed research unit and should not be pre-empted here.

## Result

PASS: R01 answers a buyer decision that the parent Plastics article does not answer in sufficient application depth.
