## 4. Stable technical fact and source contract

| ID | Permitted visible predicate | Source and exact boundary |
|---|---|---|
| `COAT-G2-C01` | A candidate should be compared with the incumbent in the actual coating system, with formulation, process, film, exposure and methods comparable or declared. Similar TDS fields, nominal TiO2 content or one result do not establish equivalence. | User-approved Coatings article, statements 001–005 and 027. No recipe, pass threshold, replacement approval or Grade inference. |
| `COAT-G2-C02` | Powder-coating method selection and interpretation depend on application, purchaser-seller agreement and often powder chemistry; many characterisation methods are relative comparisons. | ASTM D3451-24, active version updated 12 September 2024. No procedure is reproduced and no method is preferred. |
| `COAT-G2-C03` | Prepared-film microstructure and formulation-dependent pigment distribution can affect scattering and appearance observations. | NIST publication dated 1 October 2000 and Tiarks et al. 2003. These explain variables within reported systems; they do not create a universal particle-size, formulation, application or Grade rule. |
| `COAT-G2-C04` | Achieved dispersion depends on formulation and process context; opacity, gloss, roughness, rheology and observed dispersion remain separate endpoints. | Tiarks et al. 2003 and Beldarrain et al., published 13 May 2026. The latter is a bounded waterborne formulation study; no universal dispersant/process rule or single-cause diagnosis. |
| `COAT-G2-C05` | Surface-treatment and photoactivity information can frame dispersion and durability questions but pigment-level screening does not establish formulated-film service life. | van Dyk and Heyns 1998; Wang et al., published online 14 May 2010 / 2011 issue. No exterior-suitability, service-year or universal pigment-hierarchy conclusion. |
| `COAT-G2-C06` | Cost-in-use is meaningful only after the same accepted technical endpoint is reached and the buyer states the current accounting boundary. | User-approved Coatings article, statements 028–030. No saving, return, price or commercial-outcome claim. |
| `COAT-G2-C07` | Exactly eight Grades may appear as neutral Coatings relations: M-350, M-510, M-896, M-996, M-2196, M-895, M-52 and M-2377. | `PRODUCT_GRADE_APPLICATION_PROCESS_MATRIX_V0.3.csv`. Only neutral relationship and process classification render. M-996/M-2196 differentiation remains absent; no ranking, recommendation, suitability, equivalence or performance inference. |

The article's 37 hidden statement markers are audit metadata and never render. Its six source notes remain reader-visible with accurate entity, title, date and landing link. Technical claims stay attached to their stated system and study scope.

## 6. Shared-owner and conditional behaviour

- Global Chrome consumes `GLOBAL_HEADER_FOOTER_SPEC_V0.5.md`; Applications is the current navigation item and buyer-visible `CURRENT` text does not render.
- Product and Grade relations consume PRODUCT V0.3. If a Grade route is unavailable at implementation, omit that CTA without an empty card or replacement claim; do not invent another route.

- Source links render only with their complete titles. A failed downstream route or source fetch must not produce buyer-visible readiness, evidence or governance language.
