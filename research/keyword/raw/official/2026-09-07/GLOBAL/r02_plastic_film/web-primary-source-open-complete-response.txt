L100: Optical properties such as color, brightness and hiding power typically depend on pigment chemistry, concentration and dispersion in a matrix or substrate. These factors are decisive in the interaction of pigments with wavelength of visible light [cite25†6 ].
L101: Titanium dioxide (TiO_{2})—with a rutile crystal structure—is considered the most competent white pigment because its pigmentation strength is maximized by its high refractive index (2.74) [cite26†7 ] and by the lack of absorption of visible light [cite27†8 ].
L102: The hiding power—or opacity imparted by the TiO_{2}—is the capacity of pigment to hide the visual aspect of a substrate, and it can be increased by (1) avoiding the agglomeration of these particles and (2) promoting the interaction of TiO_{2} particles with wavelength at 560 nm (the center of the visible spectrum).
L103: The excellent whiteness using TiO_{2} in polymers can be achieved by maximizing the high refractive index along the three axes of rutile crystal [cite28†9 ] and the light scattering coefficient related to the diameter of the TiO_{2} particle, which is in the range of 180 to 300 nm [cite29†10 ].
L104: The energy required to optimize the dispersion of TiO_{2} pigment in polymers is related to the attraction forces between particles and affinity between components [cite30†11 ]. Dispersion of TiO_{2} into polymers is commonly obtained during melt-extrusion (ME) and depends on the mix processes, equipment (such as extruder type single, twin-screw extruder, co-rotating or counter-rotating, intermeshing or non-intermeshing) and screw design [cite31†12 ].
L105: Even the melt-flow index of the polymer matrix is another factor that plays an important role to enable the transmission of dispersion energy [cite32†13 ].
L106: Additionally, it is still possible to gain further optimization of TiO_{2} dispersion using ultrasonic radiation in the extrusion process [cite33†14 ]. This process is designated as ultrasound-assisted melt-extrusion (USME).
L107: Ultrasonic radiation produces turbulent flow conditions in a melted or liquid substance due to alternate compression and rarefaction phases of sound waves [cite34†15 ]. Therefore, USME process can increase the shear stresses in the molten polymer and promote a better homogenization of pigment in polymeric compounds.

L538@P9: Abstract
L539@P9: One of the most popular pigments used for plastic film applications is titanium dioxide, TiO2. Titania-based
L540@P9: pigments are popular because of several desirable properties. For example, TiO2 is preferred in plastics applications
L541@P9: because it is non-toxic and a relatively inert material. In addition, TiO2 does not migrate in a polymer matrix and
L542@P9: generally does not require large shear forces to disperse it into a polymer melt when properly treated. TiO2
L543@P9: morphology can be adjusted so as to attenuate different wavelengths of light for plastic end uses. This attenuation is
L544@P9: often referred to as “opacity” within the TiO2 industry. This paper describes the relationship of opacity as a function
L545@P9: of titanium dioxide concentration, the thickness of the plastic matrix and the opacity performance.
L546@P9: Introduction
L547@P9: The interaction of particles with light occurs
L548@P9: via three different pathways. The first pathway is
L549@P9: the reflectance of the light, wherein the light
L550@P9: wave does not penetrate through a particle. The
L551@P9: second pathway is the scattering of light as the
L552@P9: light penetrates the particle and is diffracted in
L553@P9: different directions. The third pathway is the
L554@P9: absorption of light by the particle. The three
L555@P9: pathways to describe light interaction with
L556@P9: particles rarely occur in isolation. Light interacts
L557@P9: with particles via all three pathways
L558@P9: simultaneously; the degree of interaction by each
L559@P9: pathway is determined by the particle chemical
L560@P9: composition and morphology. This paper
L561@P9: describes the relationship of the three pathways
L562@P9: of light interaction with particles whose design
L563@P9: in morphology and chemical composition
L564@P9: demonstrates a higher degree of scattering than
L565@P9: absorption. The description of the relationship
L566@P9: between the particle morphology and scattering
L567@P9: are presented based on the wavelength of visible
L568@P9: light. The relationship of scattering of select
L569@P9: wavelengths and path length are also highlighted
L570@P9: to demonstrate the performance if plastic articles
L571@P9: which contain particles that interact with visible
L572@P9: light.
L573@P9: Experimental:
L574@P9: All blown film samples were made by using a let
L575@P9: down of a 70% masterbatch containing
L576@P9: commercial grades of titanium dioxide. All
L577@P9: samples were analyzed with a Technidyne
L578@P9: Brightimeter™ Micro S-5. TAPPI Opacity
L579@P9: values are represented as the average of three
L580@P9: readings from various locations on the film
L581@P9: sample to monitor opacity variance in the film.
L582@P9: Table 1 highlights the data which were collected
L583@P9: from various films. Three distinctly different
L584@P9: types of titanium dioxide materials were chosen
L585@P9: based on morphology. All pigments contained an
L586@P9: organic treatment with no inorganic
L587@P9: modifications.
L588@P9: Results and Discussion:
L589@P9: The principles established by the Beer-Lambert
L590@P9: law1,2 are useful in describing the role of path
L591@P9: length for light scattering. Typically, the BeerLambert law is applied for the absorption of
L592@P9: light; but the law has parallels in scattering
L593@P9: phenomena in plastics. The refractive index
L594@P9: difference can represent the molar absorptivity.
L595@P9: Path length is the plastic thickness and
L596@P9: concentration represents the amount of pigment
L597@P9: within the matrix. The law states that there is a
L598@P9: logarithmic dependence between the
L599@P9: transmission, T, of light through a substance and
L600@P9: the product of the absorption coefficient of the
L601@P9: substance, α, and the distance the light travels
L602@P9: through the material (i.e. the path length), ℓ. As
L603@P9: the light moves through the medium, the BeerLambert law describes an exponential decay of
L604@P9: the light as it is absorbed.
L605@P9: In the plastic case, the exponential decay is also
L606@P9: noted due to the fact the light is scattered rather
L607@P9: than absorbed. The parallels of transmitted light
L608@P9: and reflected light can be noted. In the films
L609@P9: reviewed, the refractive index of the titanium
L610@P9: dioxide is not altered. Refractive index could be
L611@P9: the analogy of absorbance in the Beer-Lambert
L612@P9: law. In this study, the refractive index of the
L613@P9: titanium dioxide was not altered. Hence, the role
L614@P9: of refractive index is not under consideration in
L615@P9: this review.
L616@P9: The second factor, path length, can be noted in
L617@P9: Figure 1. The path length for the plastic film
L618@P9: samples is represented by the film thickness.
L619@P9: Increasing the path length will permit more
L620@P9: opportunities for scattering to occur; hence, the
L621@P9: TAPPI opacity is highest when film thickness is
L622@P9: greatest.
L623@P9: The third factor is the concentration of material
L624@P9: within the film which interacts with the light.
L625@P9: Again, Figure 1 highlights the phenomenon of
L626@P9: increasing concentration. As the amount of
L627@P9: titanium dioxide is increased within a given
L628@P9: thickness, the opacity climbs rapidly with the
L629@P9: initial addition. However, the rate of opacity
L630@P9: increase with titanium dioxide concentration
L631@P9: starts to decay as the system becomes less
L632@P9: efficient at scattering light.
L633@P9: The role of pigment morphology can be slightly
L634@P9: noted in the TAPPI opacity measurements (see
L635@P9: Figures 2 and 3). Pigment A and C demonstrate
L636@P9: the ability to achieve a slightly higher opacity
L637@P9: than Pigment B. However, all three pigments
L638@P9: demonstrate the behavior expected based on the
L639@P9: BeerLambert law.
L640@P9: Summary and Conclusions:
L641@P9: All three titanium dioxide materials exhibited an
L642@P9: opacity pattern that is fundamentally described
L643@P9: by the Beer-Lambert law. The law describes the
L644@P9: relationship of path length, absorptivity and
L645@P9: concentration. For the examples presented, the
L646@P9: refractive index of all titanium dioxides was
L647@P9: similar to remove the influence of extinction
L648@P9: coefficient. The only adjustments to control
L649@P9: opacity were thickness of plastic article and the
L650@P9: amount of titanium dioxide. The law describes
L651@P9: the role of path length and concentration which
L652@P9: was demonstrated for all three pigments. The law
L653@P9: serves as a useful guideline to describe the
L654@P9: expected opacity performance of plastics
L655@P9: containing titanium dioxide.

L44: This document specifies a method for the measurement of haze, an optical property resulting from wide-angle scattering of light, in transparent and substantially colourless plastics. This method is applicable to the measurement of haze values of less than 40 %.

L133: Scope
L134: 1.1 This test method covers the evaluation of specific light-transmitting and wide-angle-light-scattering properties of planar sections of materials such as essentially transparent plastic. Two procedures are provided for the measurement of luminous transmittance and haze. Procedure A uses a hazemeter as described in Section and Procedure B uses a spectrophotometer as described in Section .
L135: Material having a haze value greater than 30 % is considered diffusing and should be tested in accordance with Practice E 167.
L136: 1.2 The values stated in SI units are to be regarded as standard. Note 1For greater discrimination among materials that scatter a high percent of light within a narrow forward angle, such as is the case with abraded transparent plastics, adjust the hazemeter and perform measurements in accordance with Test Method D 1044.
