# APP-COAT Gate 2 Content Skeleton V0.1

## Page task

Help a coatings buyer define a controlled, system-specific titanium dioxide comparison, review the eight publicly supported Coatings Grade relationships without ranking, and continue to the correct Documents, Sample or RFQ task.

## Proposed visible order

### 1. Hero — Titanium Dioxide for Coatings

**Candidate copy:** Compare a candidate with the incumbent in the coating system that will actually use it. Keep the formulation basis, dispersion route, application and cure, substrate and film, exposure and endpoint methods comparable.

- Primary CTA: `Review Coatings Grades` → `#grades-to-review`
- Secondary CTA: `Build Your Comparison Basis` → `#define-the-coating`

### 2. Define the coating before comparing the Grade

Give the buyer a compact system-definition sequence: incumbent/reason for change; liquid or powder system; binder/vehicle; formulation and loading basis; additives; dispersion/process; substrate; application/cure; film; interior/exterior or other exposure; decision endpoints. State that labels frame the questions but do not select a Grade.

### 3. Read optical results in the prepared film

Explain that TiO2 optical performance is observed in the prepared coating film. Separate opacity/hiding, colour/undertone, gloss and appearance, and keep specimen, method and geometry conditions attached to each result.

### 4. Keep dispersion, rheology and appearance separate

Explain achieved dispersion as formulation- and process-specific. Ask the buyer to preserve incorporation route and process history. Keep viscosity tied to its measurement conditions and prevent one rheology or appearance result from becoming a general suitability claim.

### 5. Use surface-treatment information to frame durability questions

Explain the difference between pigment-level surface-treatment/photoactivity information and formulated-film durability. Require the intended matrix, prepared film, exposure cycle, controls and endpoints. State that accelerated exposure does not convert directly to service years.

### 6. Compare every endpoint on a declared basis

Retain the approved five-row decision table for opacity/hiding, colour/undertone, dispersion/rheology, gloss/appearance and durability. Each row states what must be comparable or declared and what the result can answer.

### 7. Compare cost only at an equal technical endpoint

Keep the buyer-owned accounting boundary: current formulation cost, coverage/film basis, process inputs, yield, waste, rework, testing and qualification. No universal saving, return or price conclusion.

### 8. Grades to Review

Show exactly eight equal, neutral Coatings relations in approved order: `M-350`, `M-510`, `M-896`, `M-996`, `M-2196`, `M-895`, `M-52`, `M-2377`. Show only the current process classification and a Grade-page action. Do not rank, differentiate, recommend or imply formulation fit.

### 9. Prepare the next request

Turn the approved article close into a compact comparison brief. Provide clear incomplete-information paths:

- multiple candidates may be named without ranking;
- unknown Grade can continue through `Not sure / Need help` in RFQ;
- required or estimated quantity in metric tonnes is accepted;
- incomplete technical conditions may be stated as unknown rather than guessed.

Then separate the three owner actions:

- `Request Documents` → `/request-documents/`: one known Grade and selected document types; human review of availability and applicable scope.
- `Request a Sample` → `/request-sample/`: application, destination and test objective for review; no sample arrangement is promised.
- `Request a Quote` → `/request-a-quote/`: application, required or estimated MT quantity, destination and known context; after submission the team reviews the details and contacts the buyer using the information provided.

### 10. Technical sources

Keep the approved six public source notes with accurate titles and direct landing links: ASTM D3451-24, NIST coating microstructure/appearance research and four identified scholarly papers. Source wording remains bounded to the claims in modules 2–5.

## Direction-sensitive boundaries

- The technical article is integrated as the page's decision guidance; its hidden statement markers do not render.
- The page uses ten modules because the buyer needs distinct system, endpoint, Grade and action decisions. It does not create Coatings child pages.
- Grade names and process labels are neutral navigation facts. Country, process, application examples and source findings do not select a Grade.
- Shared Header/Footer and downstream form copy remain with their owners.
