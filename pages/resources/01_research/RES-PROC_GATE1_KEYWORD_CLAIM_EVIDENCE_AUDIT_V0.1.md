## 4. Evidence Findings

### 4.1 Supported route facts

1. U.S. EPA and European Commission/JRC materials recognize chloride and sulfate as the two established industrial pigment-production routes.
2. The chloride route uses chlorination to form titanium tetrachloride, followed by purification and oxidation to TiO2.
3. The sulfate route dissolves titanium-bearing material into a sulfate system, hydrolyses a titanium hydrate and calcines it to TiO2.
4. Official European records support that rutile can be produced by either route; this does not establish identical grade performance.
5. Downstream grade design and treatment remain relevant after the base-pigment route and must be examined through grade-specific evidence.

### 4.2 Supported overlap finding

- Tronox states that it uses both chloride and sulfate processes for products used in coatings, plastics and décor paper.
- LB Group identifies BLR-886 as a chloride-route rutile grade for plastics.
- LB Group identifies LR-108 as a sulfate-route rutile grade for masterbatch/compounding and plastics.

These examples establish that application-family overlap exists. They do not establish equivalence, substitutability, identical performance or universal suitability.

### 4.3 Internal portfolio evidence

PRODUCT V0.3 contains 14 neutral process classifications:

- Chloride: 8 grades;
- Sulfate: 5 grades;
- Vapor-phase oxidation: 1 grade.

The matrix supports classification and owner-page relationships only. It cannot be used as evidence that one process is better, that all grades in a process behave alike, or that a buyer should select a grade.
