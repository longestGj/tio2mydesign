## 1. Identity, Audience and Intent

- **Primary audience:** industrial TiO2 procurement, sourcing and category managers comparing production-route labels during supplier or Grade screening.
- **Secondary audience:** formulation, technical-service, quality and regulatory colleagues who define qualification evidence.
- **Buyer problem:** search results often turn “chloride versus sulfate” into a universal quality ranking. Buyers need to understand what a process label establishes, what it cannot establish and which Grade-level evidence is still required.
- **Page goal:** move the buyer from a broad route comparison to a defensible Grade-evaluation plan.
- **Controlling thesis:** production route can influence how the base pigment is formed, but the route label itself is insufficient to establish a Grade-level performance conclusion.
- **Search stage:** Awareness / Technical Evaluation; the page does not become a supplier, exact-Grade, process-product or application landing page.

## 4. Facts, Evidence and Frozen Claims

Approved public evidence supports high-level route mechanics, the existence of rutile production through both routes and cited examples of overlapping application families. Public sources and their exact statement families remain those listed in Gate 2 V0.3.

The following remain prohibited:

- a universal chloride or sulfate winner;
- process-wide promises for opacity, hiding power, undertone, dispersion, durability or weatherability;
- environmental superiority without a defined boundary and current evidence;
- named-Grade equivalence, replacement or application recommendation;
- price, inventory, availability, capacity, MOQ, lead time or logistics conclusions;
- regulatory, customs, tariff or document-availability outcomes;
- any TiO2 Malaysia Grade list inferred from the route label;
- fabricated author, publisher or date metadata.

`M-996` / `M-2196` differentiation remains frozen. `CR-901` must not be forced into a chloride/sulfate binary. A missing public mapping never means “not suitable.”
