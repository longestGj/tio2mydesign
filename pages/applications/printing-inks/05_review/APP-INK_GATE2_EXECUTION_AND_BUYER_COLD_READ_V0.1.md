## 4. Most dangerous action and weakest-English checks

- **Most dangerous action:** `Request a Sample`. It states that submission starts a review and that any arrangement is confirmed separately. It does not promise availability, quantity, freight, timing or delivery.
- **RFQ boundary:** the buyer may use a known lead Grade or `Not sure / Need help`, provide a required or estimated MT quantity and list other candidates in `Additional Requirements`. The post-submit sentence says only that the team reviews requirements/details and contacts the buyer using the supplied information.
- **Most dangerous technical sentence:** the research-based dispersion paragraph. It retains aqueous, untreated primary rutile, PAAS and research-system boundaries and expressly excludes a commercial-ink, Grade, dispersant/dose and shelf-life inference.
- **Weakest-English pass:** every long sentence was reread in context. The cost sentence uses plural `printing inks` and conditions price/kg, nominal TiO2 content and loading on the same accepted technical endpoint. No internal `qualification`, `readiness`, `release`, `decision owner`, `Advance/Hold/Reject` or evidence-status language renders.
