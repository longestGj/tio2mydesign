## 1. Independent first reading: five buyer questions

### 1. Can the buyer immediately understand what this page helps them do?

**YES.** The Hero identifies a Malaysia-origin chloride-process titanium dioxide range, names the five relevant application audiences and offers two immediate paths: inspect the eight Grades or request a quotation. The page purpose is apparent without internal context.

### 2. Does the visible content answer the buyer questions required for this page type?

**YES.** The visible copy explains what the chloride-process label means and what it does not establish, presents the eight Grade choices, tells buyers what application and processing context to consider, routes them to Grade-level evidence and document requests, and explains how to start a quotation request.

Coatings, Plastics, Masterbatch, Printing Inks and Paper buyers are all explicitly represented. The application examples remain project-context prompts rather than new Grade-to-application assignments.

### 3. Can the buyer make a useful judgment and reach the next step, including when some information is still unknown?

**YES.** A buyer with a known Grade can open its product page, request its documents or include it in an RFQ. A buyer without a selected Grade is told to review all eight entries because needs can overlap, open Grade-specific evidence, and use `Not sure / Need help` in the RFQ. Application, quantity and destination give the commercial team useful context without requiring the buyer to invent a Grade choice.

### 4. Do module names, CTA labels and form guidance match what will actually happen?

**YES.** `Explore Chloride Grades` moves to the visible Grade section; `View {Grade}` opens the registered Grade page; `Compare Chloride and Sulfate Routes` hands off to the separate comparison guide; `Explore Applications` opens the Applications Hub; `Request Documents` opens a request workflow rather than promising a download; and `Request a Quote` opens the RFQ. Source-page attribution is allowed, while Grade, application, document type, quantity and destination remain buyer-entered or downstream-controlled.

### 5. Is the copy natural and clear, without internal control language, misleading expectations or unapproved promises?

**YES.** The English is concise and natural. The visible copy contains no Gate, approval, evidence-status, route-readiness, mapping, source-attribution or schema-control language. It makes no promise of universal Grade suitability, process superiority, quotation approval, price, inventory, delivery timing, automatic document release or automatic recommendation.
