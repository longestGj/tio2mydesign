## 3. SERP intent finding

The accessible results are mixed but materially coherent around three result types:

1. technical papers and patents about opacity, pigment distribution and defined polymer films;
2. TiO2-producer or additive-supplier material connecting film thickness, pigment content, dispersion, pressure development and visible defects;
3. white-masterbatch product pages aimed at blown/cast film production.

This confirms real industrial film intent, but also shows a strong cannibalization risk: many commercial pages answer “which white masterbatch should I buy?” rather than “how should a finished-film producer evaluate TiO2 performance?” R02 must own the second question and must not become a masterbatch formulation, carrier-selection or let-down recipe page.

The PVC/calendered-film result set is notably weaker and dominated by starting formulations, patents, colorant brochures and finished-vinyl product claims. It does not currently justify a second standalone article. It is useful as a controlled branch showing why evidence from blown/cast polyolefin film cannot be transferred automatically to plasticized or calendered PVC film.

## 4. Technical evidence feasibility

### 4.1 Film thickness, pigment level and opacity

The SPE Color & Appearance Division newsletter republishes a DuPont technical paper, “Relationship of Opacity with Titanium Dioxide Content & Film Thickness.” Its blown-film samples and table demonstrate, within the recorded system, that film thickness is the optical path length and TiO2 concentration is a separate variable; increasing either can change opacity, while the opacity gain from increasing concentration is nonlinear. This supports a strong explanatory core, not a universal dosing rule.

### 4.2 Dispersion, film defects and process response

The Chemours *Ti-Pure Titanium Dioxide: Plastic Films* document explicitly connects film opacity to thickness and TiO2 content, and connects masterbatch dispersion to visible specks. It also identifies equipment design, melt pressure, polymer viscosity and the TiO2 masterbatch as variables affecting the rate at which melt exits the film die. These are attributed first-party relationships and candidate questions, not independent proof or TiO2 Malaysia Grade claims.

The peer-reviewed PMC article `PMC7503362` studies defined LLDPE matrices, masterbatch concentrations and ultrasound-assisted melt extrusion. It supports the bounded relationship among polymer rheology, dispersion/agglomeration and film covering power in its exact experimental systems. It cannot establish a universal carrier, loading, screw design, treatment or process recommendation.

### 4.3 Measurement and endpoint boundaries

ASTM D1003-21 and ISO 14782:2021 public scope material show that haze and luminous-transmittance methods have defined applicability to transparent or substantially colourless plastics. They are not automatic opacity specifications for every white or opaque film. A film article must distinguish opacity/contrast, haze/transmittance, colour/undertone, visible defect count, pressure or filtration response, and mechanical/functional endpoints rather than treating them as interchangeable.

## 5. Evidence limitations

- The strongest commercial film guide is supplier-authored and must remain attributed.
- The SPE paper is a republished older conference paper; all sample conditions must travel with its data.
- The PMC study is specific to LLDPE/masterbatch/ultrasound-assisted processing.
- Public standards pages provide scope metadata, not full paid standards.
- The current capture does not establish food-contact, packaging, migration, regulatory, recyclate, sealing, printing or lamination compliance.
- Search results contain unsafe recipes, universal addition rates and grade recommendations; these are discovery evidence only and must not enter accepted claims.

## 6. Research conclusion

Evidence is sufficient for one technically substantive, buyer-facing article about evaluating opacity, dispersion, processing and visible film quality at a defined film thickness and construction. It is not sufficient for universal formulation guidance or a standalone PVC/calendered-film article.
