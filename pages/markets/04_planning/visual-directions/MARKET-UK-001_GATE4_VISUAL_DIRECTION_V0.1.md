## 1. Direction decision

### 1.1 Recommended direction — UK Procurement Editorial

The page extends the approved TiO2 Malaysia industrial editorial system and the sibling European Union Market `Procurement Editorial` precedent. Its UK-specific visual signature is the transition from supplier and application discovery into the Great Britain / Northern Ireland destination decision, followed by document, origin, current-import-source and quotation paths.

It must not resemble a tourism page, flag directory, compliance dashboard, distributor locator, warehouse page or logistics-coverage map.

The visual hierarchy is:

1. supplier-led Hero and three approved actions;
2. direct procurement answer;
3. Applications before representative Grades;
4. equally legible Great Britain and Northern Ireland assessment paths;
5. six-step supply-request preparation;
6. document qualification and Malaysia-origin context;
7. source-led current UK import checks;
8. Buyer Questions and terminal quotation action.

### 1.2 Alternatives considered

| Direction | Benefit | Risk | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. UK Procurement Editorial | Preserves the approved buyer path and aligns with the EU sibling without copying EU content | Requires disciplined typography across a long page | Recommended |
| B. GB/NI compliance dashboard | Makes the territory split immediately prominent | Overstates compliance certainty and turns guidance into a status interface | Rejected |
| C. UK logistics/coverage landing page | Familiar supplier-page pattern | Can imply local stock, delivery reach, warehousing or market availability | Rejected |

## 3. Page-specific components

### 3.1 Hero

- Desktop retains the approved 8/4 supplier-led split.
- The H1 remains the dominant editorial signal; the right panel contains only the three approved actions.
- The right panel is a quiet Soft Background decision surface, not a promotional illustration.
- Tablet and Mobile stack the action surface after the Hero copy.

### 3.2 Direct answer

- White surface on Soft Background with a 4px accessible Teal left rule.
- It remains an explanation block rather than a warning, alert or internal-status panel.

### 3.3 Applications and representative Grades

- Five Applications use equal, text-led cards with no colour-coded priority.
- Applications remain visually upstream of Grades.
- Grades use two equal category panels with three flat Grade rows each.
- Grade name is the primary label; approved relationship text and action are secondary.
- No badges, stars, rankings, recommendation ribbons, UK suitability markers or process icons are used.

### 3.4 Great Britain / Northern Ireland

- Two equal destination panels retain a shared component language.
- Great Britain uses a Navy top rule; Northern Ireland uses an accessible Teal top rule.
- Colour distinguishes orientation but not approval, completeness, priority or recommendation.
- Territory names and regulatory text do the semantic work; no flags, maps or availability graphics are introduced.
- Mobile stacks Great Britain before Northern Ireland and preserves complete copy and source links.

### 3.5 Supply-request checklist and Documents

- Six numbered checklist cards use flat borders and Teal numbering.
- Documents remain a separate 2×2 qualification grid on wide screens and stack responsively.
- No fake PDF thumbnails, seals, certificate graphics or availability badges appear.

### 3.6 Origin, Trade and questions

- Malaysia Origin uses one Navy trust band without photography or tourism cues.
- Trade uses a Soft Background editorial field, a strong Navy rule and two restrained official-source actions.
- It visualises no tariff, duty, exemption or trade-remedy outcome.
- Buyer Questions use clean disclosure rows rather than individual cards; two open rows prove answer rhythm.

### 3.7 Final conversion

- Final RFQ uses Deep Navy, one accessible Teal primary action, one white outline action and one text action.
- It remains visually separate from the shared Footer while forming a composed dark-page closure.

## 5. Media direction

Gate 4 remains image-free. Prohibited imagery includes flags, maps, UK landmarks, ports, shipping routes, office pins, warehouse markers, stock indicators, factory photography, certificate artwork and generic business people.

The absence of imagery is intentional: typography, spacing, rules, structured destinations, product paths and official-source blocks carry the procurement story without creating unsupported supply or regulatory implications.
