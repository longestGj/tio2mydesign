## 1. Chosen Narrative Approach

### Selected: Buyer decision path

The article begins with the technically corrected answer, explains the two formation routes only to the depth needed for interpretation, defines the evidence limit of a route label, and then moves the reader into grade-level evaluation and application-relevant testing.

### Rejected alternatives

| Approach | Reason not selected |
|---|---|
| “Which process is better?” comparison table | Encourages universal rankings and label-only performance conclusions unsupported by evidence |
| Process-science-first long article | Can be accurate but delays the procurement answer and risks competing with PRODUCT-PROC-CL / PRODUCT-PROC-SU |
| Product catalogue by route | Invades Products/Grade ownership and turns a research page into a commercial landing page |

## 2. Controlling Thesis

All visible copy, tables, FAQs, metadata and machine-readable content must preserve this exact distinction:

> The production route can influence how the base pigment is formed, but the route label alone is insufficient to establish a grade-level performance conclusion.

This means the article must not claim either that one route is universally better or that production route has no technical influence.

## 6. Module-level Content Skeleton

### Module 5 — Two Routes, Two Formation Paths

#### H2

`How the Chloride and Sulfate Routes Differ`

#### Chloride route summary

`Titanium-bearing feedstock → chlorination → titanium tetrachloride purification → oxidation → base TiO2 → finishing/treatment`

#### Sulfate route summary

`Titanium-bearing feedstock → sulfuric-acid digestion/dissolution → titanyl sulfate → hydrolysis → hydrated TiO2 → calcination → base TiO2 → finishing/treatment`

#### Required qualifier

The diagram is a simplified orientation, not a complete plant description. Route chemistry and formation conditions can be technically relevant; later grade design, treatment and production control also matter to the material ultimately evaluated by the buyer.

#### Prohibited content

- no plant-efficiency or cost ranking;
- no emissions/waste comparison;
- no factory or Malaysia capability claim;
- no “cleaner”, “premium” or “traditional/obsolete” label.

### Module 6 — What Route Can Influence—and What the Label Alone Cannot Establish

#### H2

`What the Route Can Indicate—and What the Label Cannot Prove Alone`

#### Column A: route can provide context for

- the chemistry used to separate and form the TiO2 base pigment;
- the intermediate and formation path;
- some questions a technical team may ask about crystal formation and subsequent processing; and
- why the route remains a relevant item in a complete grade record.

#### Column B: route label alone is insufficient for

- exact opacity or hiding performance;
- undertone, whiteness or colour response in the buyer's system;
- weatherability or durability in a defined end use;
- dispersion or processing behavior in a specific resin, vehicle or process;
- regulatory/document applicability;
- grade equivalence, substitution or commercial suitability.

#### Bridge sentence direction

Do not ignore the route; do not stop at the route. The next decision must be made from the exact grade evidence and the buyer's application conditions.

### Module 7 — Evaluate the Grade, Not the Label Alone

#### H2

`Which Grade-Level Evidence Should Buyers Compare?`

| Evaluation question | Evidence to compare | Required caution |
|---|---|---|
| Opacity and hiding | Current grade-specific optical data, declared method and a formulation-relevant comparison | Do not compare values from incompatible methods or formulations as if equivalent |
| Undertone and whiteness | Grade-specific colour/undertone data with method, reference and test conditions | Do not assign a universal tone to the route |
| Weatherability and durability | Surface-treatment information plus relevant accelerated or outdoor exposure evidence | Test type, substrate, binder/system and duration must be known |
| Dispersion and processability | Grade treatment, relevant physical data and evidence in the buyer's resin/vehicle/process window | Application-family overlap does not prove identical processing behavior |
| Thermal/process stability | Grade-specific evidence for the intended temperature and process conditions | Route label alone does not establish suitability |
| Evidence comparability | Current TDS/source date, test method, units, typical-versus-specification status and sample identity | “Typical” is not automatically a guaranteed specification |

This is an evaluation framework, not a public product specification table.

### Module 8 — Application Overlap Is Not Equivalence

#### H2

`Can Both Routes Serve the Same Application Family?`

#### Answer direction

Yes, overlap can occur. First-party manufacturer information shows chloride-route and sulfate-route rutile grades positioned for plastics, and one producer states that it uses both routes across coatings, plastics and décor paper. This demonstrates overlap in application families, not identical performance or one-to-one substitution.

#### Evidence cards

1. **Chloride example:** LB Group BLR-886 — rutile TiO2 produced by the chloride process for plastics.
2. **Sulfate example:** LB Group LR-108 — rutile TiO2 produced by the sulfate process for masterbatch/compounding and plastics.
3. **Cross-route portfolio example:** Tronox — both processes used for products serving coatings, plastics and décor paper.

Competitor grade names serve only as transparent citations for the overlap proposition. They are not compared against TiO2 Malaysia grades and do not create alternative/equivalent intent.

### Module 9 — Five-step qualification workflow

#### H2

`A Five-Step Route-to-Grade Qualification Workflow`

1. **Define the application and decision criteria.** Record the formulation, substrate, process window and performance questions that matter.
2. **Identify the exact candidate grade and route.** Treat the route as one field in the grade record, not the conclusion.
3. **Collect current grade-specific evidence.** Obtain relevant technical data, treatments, methods, dates and evidence status from the correct owner/source.
4. **Normalize the comparison.** Align units, methods, conditions and typical-versus-specification status before comparing values.
5. **Validate in the intended system.** Use application-relevant laboratory or production testing before qualification, substitution or scale-up decisions.

#### Decision outcomes

- `CONTINUE_TO_TEST`
- `REQUEST_MISSING_EVIDENCE`
- `HOLD_COMPARISON`

These are internal reader decisions expressed in buyer-facing language; the literal status codes must not appear on the page.

## 8. Buyer Questions Skeleton

### Q1. Which process makes better titanium dioxide?

Answer direction: no universal winner is established by the process label. Route can influence base-pigment formation, but the buyer still needs exact grade evidence and application-relevant testing.

### Q2. Can both chloride and sulfate routes produce rutile TiO2?

Answer direction: yes, rutile can be produced through either route. This does not make grades interchangeable.

### Q3. Does the same production route mean two grades are equivalent?

Answer direction: no. Equivalence requires defined criteria, comparable methods and validation in the intended formulation/process.

### Q4. Which route gives higher opacity, a bluer undertone or better weather resistance?

Answer direction: the route label alone is insufficient to establish those grade-level conclusions. Compare current grade-specific optical, colour, treatment and durability evidence under relevant conditions.

FAQ is included because these questions remain after the main comparison and map to observed search intent. It is not inherited from another page, and visible FAQ does not authorize `FAQPage` Schema.
