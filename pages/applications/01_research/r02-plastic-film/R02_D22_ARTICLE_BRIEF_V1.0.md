## 2. Working title direction

**Titanium Dioxide for Plastic Film: How to Compare Opacity, Dispersion, and Processing at a Defined Film Thickness**

The exact title may be refined if the reader, owner, decision and scope remain unchanged.

## 3. Primary reader and decision

Film fabricators, converters, extrusion technical teams, application laboratories, quality teams and technical procurement teams comparing a pigmentary TiO2 input or TiO2-bearing candidate for a defined white or opaque film construction.

Help the reader decide what evidence is needed before a candidate enters a matched film evaluation at a declared polymer/carrier, construction, process, thickness and end-use requirement.

## 4. Required explanatory argument

1. A “film grade,” pigment TDS or white-masterbatch claim does not establish performance in a finished film.
2. Film opacity is a system result: explain the bounded relationship among optical scattering, TiO2 concentration/distribution and film thickness/path length.
3. Explain why opacity gains with concentration or thickness are not universal linear dosage rules.
4. Connect dispersion/agglomeration and material/process history to visible specks or other film defects without claiming one universal defect method or threshold.
5. Explain how equipment design, polymer rheology, masterbatch identity/dispersion, pressure or filtration response and processing history can affect comparability and film quality.
6. Separate initial colour/undertone, opacity/contrast, haze/transmittance, visible defects, process response and mechanical/functional endpoints; do not use one as a proxy for all.
7. Define a matched comparison: same declared resin/carrier and let-down basis, film construction, thickness/measurement basis, process conditions, control/incumbent, conditioning, methods and endpoints.
8. Explain the boundary between finished-film evaluation and masterbatch manufacture/qualification.
9. Include PVC/calendered film only as a controlled branch showing non-transfer across polymer, plasticizer/stabilizer and process contexts.
10. End with a concise buyer input checklist for current documents or a controlled film trial.

## 5. Scope boundaries

- One Plastic Film article; no standalone PVC/calendered-film article.
- Blown and cast film may be direct contexts; do not claim they are interchangeable.
- Use PE/LLDPE, PVC or other study systems only within exact source conditions.
- Do not turn this into PE-versus-PP selection; that belongs to R04.
- Do not teach concentrate formulation, carrier selection, high-loading compounding or masterbatch manufacture; those belong to APP-MB/R19.
- Printing, sealing, lamination, food contact, migration, agriculture, hygiene and packaging are only requirements to define unless D22 produces adequate exact evidence.
- Do not expand into nano-TiO2 functional films, photocatalytic films, coatings on film or consumer/cosmetic uses.

## 6. Product boundary

Do not name, recommend, rank or imply film suitability for any TiO2 Malaysia Grade. APP-PLAS neutral Grade mappings do not prove film fit. Historic film wording for individual Grades is a research lead only unless the current governed evidence chain independently authorizes that exact claim.

## 7. Prohibited claims

- universal TiO2/masterbatch dosage, let-down ratio, carrier, screw design, temperature, throughput or filter-pressure threshold;
- named-Grade recommendation, direct equivalence or guaranteed replacement;
- guaranteed opacity, whiteness, dispersion, absence of specks/lacing, film strength, printability, sealability, migration, lifespan, savings or downgauging;
- food-contact, packaging, migration, regulatory or certification acceptance without exact current authority;
- extrapolation of a supplier graph, one LLDPE study, one PVC formulation or one thickness to unlike systems;
- rutile/anatase, treatment, process route or particle-size label as sufficient proof of candidate performance.

## 8. Evidence requirements

D22 must perform its own governed A02 research. D23's evidence is a frozen scope/feasibility input, not a substitute.

Priority lanes:

- peer-reviewed polymer-film research for dispersion, opacity and system dependence;
- technical society or standards sources for thickness, optical methods and endpoint meaning;
- producer application material for film-specific variables, clearly attributed;
- current official authority if any regulatory/food-contact/migration statement is retained.

Every numeric value, graph-derived statement or study result must retain exact polymer, film thickness, concentration, process, method and source conditions.

## 9. Reader-quality requirement

The finished article must be explanation-led. Any comparison sequence or checklist is subordinate. Do not expose internal governance vocabulary, evidence state machines, gate/owner/route/handoff mechanics or a package-control architecture. The reader should remember the film technical relationships and non-transfer boundaries—not an SOP.
