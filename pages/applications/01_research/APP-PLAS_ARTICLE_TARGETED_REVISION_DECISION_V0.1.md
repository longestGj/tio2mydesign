## 2. Article direction

Working title:

> Titanium Dioxide for Plastics: How to Select the Right Grade

Primary reader:

Plastics converters, compound or masterbatch technical teams, technical procurement teams and application laboratories that already use pigmentary TiO2 and need to shortlist or compare a supplier or replacement grade for a defined polymer application.

Core reader question:

> How should a plastics buyer select and compare TiO2 grades according to resin, processing route, exposure and the failure condition that matters, without treating similar TDS fields or a general “plastic grade” label as proof of equivalence?

The article should teach one durable selection model:

`Resin → Process → Exposure → Failure condition → Candidate comparison → Controlled application trial → Cost at accepted output`

This is an article model, not a D23 page structure or an internal approval workflow.

## 3. What to preserve from the existing article

Preserve and improve the strongest existing content:

1. why “plastic grade” is not one universal grade;
2. optical performance in the resin/specimen rather than powder whiteness alone;
3. achieved dispersion, FPV/screen behavior and the need to separate pigment effects from gels or contamination;
4. surface treatment as an application trade-off rather than a simple quality ranking;
5. the effect of carrier, let-down, loading route, heat history and moisture-sensitive polymers;
6. distinct selection paths for high-loading masterbatch, film, PVC/outdoor plastics and engineering polymers;
7. method-aware TDS comparison;
8. controlled incumbent-versus-candidate application trials;
9. cost-in-use at the same accepted output;
10. the information a supplier needs to discuss a bounded candidate and sample comparison.

The application routes may remain substantial because they are genuinely different buyer contexts. Avoid turning each route into a separate mini-SOP or repeating the same qualification disclaimer after every paragraph.
