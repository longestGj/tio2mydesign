## 2. Approved article subject

Working title:

> Titanium Dioxide for Printing Inks: How to Compare Opacity, Dispersion, and Print Performance

Primary reader:

Printing-ink formulators, technical procurement teams, converters and application laboratories evaluating a titanium dioxide supplier or replacement pigment for a defined white-ink system and printing process.

Core reader question:

> How should a printing-ink buyer compare TiO2 grades in the actual ink, printing process, substrate, deposited film and end-use conditions without treating similar powder/TDS values or a generic “ink grade” description as proof of equivalent print performance?

The article should teach this decision sequence:

`Ink chemistry and printing process → Substrate and deposited film → Optical target → Dispersion/rheology and process behavior → Drying or cure and finished print → Controlled incumbent comparison → Cost per accepted printed output`

This sequence is the article's buyer logic. It is not a D23 page architecture, HTML structure or internal Gate workflow.

## 3. Required article value

The article should answer the buyer's real selection questions in connected expert prose.

### 3.1 Define the system before the pigment ranking

Explain why the same TiO2 input may behave differently when the binder/vehicle, solvent or water phase, energy-curing context where relevant, dispersant/additives, pigment loading, milling/dispersion route, viscosity window, printing process, anilox/plate/screen or other transfer conditions, substrate, deposited film, drying/cure and end-use requirements change.

Distinguish flexographic, gravure, screen, offset, inkjet or other printing contexts only where evidence supports a useful TiO2 selection difference. Do not imply that every process is equally relevant to pigmentary TiO2 or that one article supplies a universal formulation.

### 3.2 Explain optical performance in the printed result

Explain that opacity/hiding, whiteness, undertone, colour strength, gloss and visual appearance belong to the prepared ink and printed film on a declared substrate and film-weight/coverage basis. Similar TiO2 content, particle-size descriptions or powder colour do not establish equal opacity or print appearance.

Make clear how meaningful comparisons keep ink basis, TiO2 loading, substrate, deposited film, printing/application method and measurement reference comparable. Avoid unsupported universal particle-size or loading rules.

### 3.3 Treat dispersion, rheology and storage behavior as related but distinct

Explain achieved dispersion in the actual ink vehicle and process rather than treating “easy dispersion” as an intrinsic universal label. Where evidence supports it, discuss agglomerates, milling or energy input, particle-size distribution, settling/sedimentation, redispersibility, viscosity/rheology, filtration and print-transfer effects.

Do not claim that TiO2 is the sole cause of viscosity drift, settling, plugging, wear, poor transfer, specks or print defects. Resin/binder, dispersant, other pigments/fillers, contaminants, formulation balance, storage and equipment may contribute.

Abrasion or equipment-wear discussion may be included only if supported by appropriately scoped evidence and tied to a defined ink/printing process. It must not become a generic TiO2 hazard claim.

### 3.4 Connect pigment comparison to the finished print

Show how a controlled evaluation can separate:

- optical outcome on the selected substrate;
- achieved dispersion and rheology;
- transfer/printability within the intended process window;
- drying or cure response;
- adhesion, rub/abrasion resistance, gloss, blocking or other finished-print properties only where they are actually relevant and evidence-supported;
- storage stability and redispersibility where required;
- the buyer's end-use and destination-document requirements.

The article must not convert one laboratory ink drawdown, grind reading, viscosity value or printed sample into universal application approval.

### 3.5 Compare documents and economics correctly

Explain how a TDS can support shortlisting but cannot establish equivalent performance when methods, vehicles, references or test conditions differ. Current SDS, lot CoA and destination/application documents should be requested according to the buyer's needs without claiming universal regulatory approval.

Compare economics only after technically comparable output is achieved. The commercial basis should be cost per accepted printed output or another buyer-defined accepted-output unit, including only measured or documented formulation, process, waste/rework, coverage and qualification differences. Do not claim a saving or ROI without buyer data.

## 4. Suggested article shape

D22 may refine headings for natural reading, but the article should generally move through:

1. direct answer and selection model;
2. define the ink, process, substrate and print target;
3. optical performance in the deposited ink film;
4. dispersion, rheology and storage/process behavior;
5. relevant differences between printing/ink contexts;
6. TDS and method-aware comparison;
7. controlled incumbent-versus-candidate trial;
8. cost at accepted printed output;
9. buyer information needed for documents and a sample comparison;
10. concise technical source notes.

One compact comparison table or short FAQ may be used when it adds distinct decision value. Do not pad the article with repeated disclaimers, a long generic checklist or many near-duplicate FAQs. Target approximately 2,400–3,400 visible English words when supported by evidence; quality and completeness take precedence over reaching a number.

## 5. Evidence requirements

Use concise reader-usable source notes for material technical mechanisms and method boundaries. Prefer current standards, scientific/government institutions, peer-reviewed publications and other accountable technical sources. Manufacturer literature may be used only for narrowly attributed product/portfolio or method context and must not be the principal authority or evidence of TiO2 Malaysia capability.

Evidence should support, as applicable:

- TiO2 optical/scattering behavior in pigmented systems;
- dispersion or particle/agglomerate behavior in printing-ink vehicles;
- rheology, settling/storage or print-transfer implications when claimed;
- method boundaries for printed opacity, colour, gloss, viscosity, grind/dispersion or other named tests;
- process-specific or cured/dried-print statements;
- any abrasion/wear, inkjet or regulatory statement retained in the article.

If suitable evidence is not available, omit or narrow the claim. Do not use competitor wording, structure, grade ranking or unsupported application claims.

The business deliverable is the article. Do not create a D23 evidence register, page Gate package, SEO plan, HTML specification or approval package.

## 6. Claim and scope boundaries

- Do not invent formulations, recipes, dispersant packages, dosages, loading ranges, milling energy/time, viscosity windows, particle-size thresholds, test values, print speeds, cure settings, shelf life or performance numbers.
- Do not assert a universal best grade, compatibility, equivalence, drop-in replacement, process improvement, wear reduction, opacity gain, cost saving, regulatory approval or guarantee.
- Do not name or recommend a TiO2 Malaysia grade unless D23 separately supplies an approved product relationship and exact public claim. Named grades are not required.
- Do not present TiO2 Malaysia as a finished-ink manufacturer, printing converter, testing laboratory or regulatory adviser.
- Do not take exact grade, country-supplier, sulfate/chloride process, printing-process subtopic or diagnostic-resource keywords as the article's primary target.
- Do not begin another article in parallel.

## 7. Natural buyer close

End by asking the reader to prepare:

- current TiO2 grade and reason for considering a change;
- ink type, binder/vehicle, solvent/water/curing context, formulation and TiO2 loading basis;
- dispersion/milling route, equipment and relevant process history;
- printing method, transfer/application conditions and normal process window;
- substrate, deposited film/coverage basis, drying or cure and end-use exposure;
- optical, rheological, storage, printability and finished-print targets that actually decide acceptance;
- methods, current results, observed problem and rejection conditions;
- required current TDS, SDS, lot/quality, destination and commercial documents;
- sample quantity and controlled comparison needs.

Invite a request for current technical documents and a controlled incumbent-versus-candidate sample/print comparison. Do not decide the future site CTA label, form, destination or response promise.
